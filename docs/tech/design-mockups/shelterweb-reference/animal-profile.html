His story

Fourteen months of being the easy dog everyone overlooks

Duke arrived as a stray in early summer, thin and unsure of doorways. Fourteen months later he is a different dog: settled indoors, polite on the lead, happy to nap through an entire afternoon.

He knows sit, down and wait, and he walks without pulling — most days he checks in with a soft glance at every corner. What he does not want is another dog in the house. He is not aggressive, he is simply uninterested, and forcing the issue is unfair to him.

That single line on his file — must be the only pet — is the whole reason he is still here. In every other respect he is easy. He would love a household where someone is home most evenings, a sofa he is allowed on, and a walk that ends in a long, quiet lean against your legs.

He leans his whole weight against your leg the moment you stop walking — like he's making sure you're not leaving without him. Maya R. · kennel team

Care & medical

The practical side of living with Duke

Exercise

Two steady 30-minute walks a day. He sniffs more than he sprints, and is happiest at an even pace.

Diet

Adult large-breed formula, two measured meals a day. We'll send his current portion chart home with you.

Grooming

Short coat — a weekly brush and a monthly nail trim keep him tidy. Low maintenance.

Medical

Neutered, vaccinated and microchipped. Mild seasonal allergies, managed with a quick wipe-down after walks.
